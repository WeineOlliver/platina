The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Card Collector Guide

    • Introduction to Card Collector Trophy

    Welcome to this guide dedicated to  Card Collector, a single trophy for collecting all Gwent Cards. If you're a fan of digital card games, such as Hearthstone: Heroes of Warcraft, you'll probably immediately be drawn to Gwent in The Witcher 3 from the first opponent you face.

    You'll soon find out, however, that trying to get a full collection of Gwent cards is actually pretty tough seeing as a lot are extremely missable. Aside from your basic starting deck, the cards you can obtain are split into two indirect categories; cards tied to specific quests, characters, or sellers; and cards won randomly from merchants, armorers, blacksmiths etc.

    Including your base deck, and the new cards added with the first expansion Hearts of Stone, there are currently 207 Gwent cards in the game. An amount of these are duplicates (eg there exist three copies of the card Commander's Horn that you can obtain) however only one of each card is required for the trophy. There are 195 cards without including the cards added from Hearts of Stone, and these are the ones this guide will be based around.

    If you wish to read a very in depth guide about the game of Gwent, read the next few sections. If not, and you are here solely for the trophy information, skip to Preparation.

    Introduction to Gwent

    Gwent itself is a very simplistic and fiendishly addictive card game that exists as a mini-game within The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, apparently replacing poker from the previous games. Here the game will be broken down for you and the mechanics will be explained, because you will need to play a lot of Gwent throughout the game in order to obtain every card and get that, annoyingly only bronze,  Card Collector trophy.

    The game of Gwent is created to represent armies going to war, putting you in charge of a commander and having units at your disposal. Every card in Gwent falls under four (five technically) factions; Northern Realms, Nilfgaardian Empire, Monsters, and Scoia'tael. There are five factions technically because there are cards in the game that are neutral, that is they do not fall under any of the pre-mentioned four factions. You can have four decks in the game, one for each of the four factions. Each faction's deck can only contain cards from that specific faction and any neutral cards you wish.

    The coloured banner on the left side of each card symbolises which faction it belongs to. Neutral cards have no banner.

    How to Play Gwent

    The objective of Gwent is to win two rounds in one game before your opponent does. On the middle-left of the screen are four red crystals, which will be destroyed as you or your opponent lose rounds.

    In Gwent you will take the role of a commander. There is a different commander for each of the four factions, and each commander has five different cards to choose from (only four for each commander required for Card Collector; Hearts of Stone added a new card for each commander).

    The four commanders are:
    1. Northern Realms - Foltest
    2. Nilfgaardian Empire - Emhyr var Emreis
    3. Monsters - Eredin
    4. Scoia'tael - Francesca Findabair

    When building your deck, you can select which card you would prefer to use for your commander, provided you have collected them of course. Each commander card has a unique ability that can be used once per game. That is, not once per round (there are multiple rounds in each game of Gwent), but once per entire game, so use it wisely. So while each commander has four (five including Hearts of Stone) cards to choose from, each has its own ability, so each of Foltest's commander cards will have a different one-time use ability, same with Emhyr, Eredin, and Francesca.

    An example is shown below of one card for each commander:
     
     
     
     

    Each commander card only contains flavour text and does not display what the card actually does. Viewing each card in your collection will display its ability, though, and hovering over the card while in a match and pressing the trigger to inspect the card will show its ability.

    Side note: I have observed during my game and after obtaining these images that it seems that, in the game itself, the art work for some of the commander cards has been swapped, but everything else about the card has stayed the same (e.g. the image I have shown for Foltest King of Temeria has the artwork for the card Foltest The Siegemaster in my game) so do not worry if you notice discrepencies in artwork, if ever researching commander cards.

    The Gwent board is split up into three sections for you and three for your opponent, each representing a class of units. These three sections are:
    1. Close combat
    2. Ranged
    3. Siege

    Each unit card in your deck will belong to one of these three sections, represented by the symbol on the top left of the card. A sword represents close combat, a bow for ranged, and catapult looking thing for siege.

    Each unit card has a power value, displayed just above the unit's combat section in the very top left of the card. Obviously the higher the power value, the better. Each game of Gwent starts with a coin flip to determine who will go first. The person who goes first plays a single card, then it switches to the other person who plays a single card, and this repeats. When you play a unit, it will go to its designated area on the board and its power value will be added to your overall power value for that section, as displayed by the number on the left of each section. The overall power value for each of the three sections will be added together to form your overall score for that round, shown on the left of the screen also. The aim of the game is win two rounds against your opponent in the match, which is done by having the higher overall round score at the round's end. The only way for a round to end is for both players to run out of cards to play, or for both players to pass and progress to the next round, which you as a player do by holding  during your turn. Once both players have ran out of cards or both have passed, both players' overall round score will be compared and whoever's is highest wins the round.

    At the beginning of a game of Gwent, you draw your starting hand. If you do not like the cards you get, you have the opportunity before the match begins to put up to two of these cards back into your deck and draw replacements, a la mullinganing in Hearthstone. Be careful, however, as unlike Hearthstone, Magic: The Gathering, or other well known card games, you do not draw a card at the start of every turn, so the hand of cards you begin the match with (after replacing up to two if you wish) is your hand for the entirety of the match, no matter how many rounds you play. There are specific cards designed to help you draw cards, but that is a topic for later.

    At the end of each round, all cards on both players' sides of the board are sent to their respective discard pile.

    So essentially, draw a hand, replace up to two if you wish, and get a higher score than your opponent. You have to be strategic in how you play, for example you don't want to go completely all out in the first round and use every card in your hand to beat your opponent, and then have nothing for the next rounds and your opponent beats you by playing literally their weakest card. Sometimes passing and saving your cards can be a good thing. Your deck must contain a minimum number of 22 unit cards, but can contain as many as you wish passed that.

    Gwent Mechanics

    Obviously it is not just as simple as that; Gwent has multiple different card mechanics which change how you play, so let's dive straight in.


    Topic 1: Weather 

    In the game there exists four weather cards. These are special neutral cards that can be used in any deck, and do not count as unit cards. The four cards are as follows:
     
     
     
     

    In the middle-left of the board is a small space reserved for weather cards. Once played by either player, the card is placed in that space and affects both players. Each weather card affects specific sections (close combat, ranged, siege) of the board, and their details are listed below:
    • Biting Frost - Changes the power value on all close combat units to 1.
    • Impenetrable Fog - Changes the power value on all ranged units to 1.
    • Torrential Rain - Changes the power value on all siege units to 1.
    • Clear Weather - Destroys any weather cards currently in affect and removes their effects.
     


    Topic 2: Commander's Horn

    Commander's Horn is a handy ability that even has its own card tied to it, but can also be found on specific unit cards. The card Commander's Horn is shown below:
     

    The symbol for the Commander's Horn ability is shown as the horn in the top left of this card. Unit cards can also have abilities, as shown below, and can have the Commander's Horn ability if the horn symbol is shown on them.

    When either player plays Commander's Horn they choose one of their three board sections and the card is placed to the left of this seciton. Whilst in play, Commander's Horn doubles the power value of all units in that section, only on their side of the board. This card cannot be destroyed and remains for the entirety of the round it was used in. 

    If a unit has the Commander's Horn ability, it doubles the power value of all other units in the same section on its user's side of the board.



    Topic 3: Scorch

    Scorch is an ability that has its own card, like the Commander's Horn ability, and can also be found on specific unit cards. The card Scorch is shown below:
     

    The symbol for the Scorch ability is displayed in the top left of this card, the skull. When units also have the Scorch ability, it will be displayed on their card.

    When a player uses the Scorch card, the strongest unit on the field is killed, regardless of whose it is, and then Scorch is sent to the discard pile. If the strongest single unit value on the board is held by multiple units when Scorch is used, all those units are destroyed. 

    If a unit has the Scorch ability, it destroys your opponent's strongest unit in the same section of the board that your Scorch minion is played, but only if your opponent's total power value for that section is 10 or above. Meaning if you play that unit and your opponent only has a total value of 8 in that section, the unit will remain on your board but its Scorch ability will have been wasted.



    Topic 4: Decoy

    Decoy is a very interesting ability, only held by the Decoy card as shown below:
     

    The decoy ability is shown in the top left of the card as a target dummy.

    Once a player uses Decoy they select one unit on their side of the board and swap it with Decoy. The unit in choice returns to the player's hand and Decoy takes their place on the board. Of course, Decoy itself has no power value so whatever power value the minion you selected had will be subtracted from your overall power value for its section.

    There are numerous reasons for doing this, but they will be touched on below. Just know that Decoy is perhaps one of the best cards in the game, and there are three to obtain.



    Topic 5: Tight Bond

    Now we are onto abilities only held by units. When a unit has an ability, it will be displayed as an icon on the left side of the card below its power value and board section icon. We have previously learned Commander's Horn and Scorch are displayed as a horn and skull respectively, but now we look at the others.

    Tight Bond is an ability only held by units of which there exist multiple copies in the game, so unique cards will never have the Tight Bond ability. A card with Tight Bond is displayed below:
     

    Tight Bond is displayed as two hands on a unit. When two or more units of the same name with the Tight Bonds ability are on a player's side of the board at the same time, each card involved in the Tight Bond has its power value multiplied by however many units are involved.

    If these cards are buffed with the Commander's Horn ability, their power value is first increased by Tight Bond, and then further doubled by Commander's Horn. However, if any of the units involved in the Tight Bond are removed from the board, the other units in the Tight Bond will have their power value adjusted depending on how many there are now.



    Topic 6: Muster

    Muster is an ability only found within the Monsters and Scoia'tael factions, so Northern Realms and Nilfgaardian Empire miss this one out.

    A card with Muster is displayed below:
     

    The Muster ability is displayed as two helmets. When a unit with Muster is played, the player's deck is searched for any units with the same name as the one played, and plays them on the player's board. 

    There exist some cards in the game with the same name but different artwork, such as the Nekker card shown above; there are three in the game, each with a different artwork, but all with the same name, so Muster works with these. There are also cards with a different subtitle, so there exist Vampire: Bruxa, Vampire: Garkain, and numerous other vampires. These all work with Muster as well seeing as they all have the main Vampire name. The same applies to the Crone cards; there are three, each with a different subtitle, but all with the name Crone and the Muster ability.



    Topic 7: Medic

    Medic is an extremely useful ability, shown on a card below:
     

    Medic is displayed on a card as a heart with a plus in the middle. When a unit with Medic is played on a player's board, that player selects one unit card in their discard pile and plays it to their board.

    Medic is one of the abilities that works very well with Decoy; play your Medic card, revive someone, play Decoy and swap it with your Medic, then play the Medic again and get another revive.



    Topic 8: Spy

    Spy is one of the best cards in the game, shown on a card below:
     

    Spy is displayed as an eye on a unit. When a Spy unit is played, the card is actually played on your opponent's side of the board, and its power is given to them. However, the person who played the Spy draws two cards from their deck, a crucial ability in a game where you don't draw cards at the start of each turn.

    Spy is another ability that works great with Decoy; if your opponent plays a card with Spy, they draw two cards and the Spy card is played on your side of the board. However, you can use Decoy to swap with that Spy card, and then play that Spy card whenever you want to get two cards.

    At the end of a round, if a Spy was on the board, it will be sent to the discard pile of whatever side of the board it was on, not whoever played it last.



    Topic 9: Agile

    Agile is another ability only found in Monsters and Scoia'tael, displayed on a card below:
     

    The Agile ability is shown as two curving arrows, but you may also notice this card, Filavandrel aén Fidháil, does not have a static board section (close combat, ranged, siege) but instead has both close combat and ranged in its symbol.

    When you play a unit with the Agile ability, you can choose which section of the board to place it in from the two sections displayed on the card, so playing Filavandrel aén Fidháil allows you to play the card in either your close combat or ranged sections.

    When a unit with Agile is revived with the Medic ability, it will return to the section it was originally in when it was sent to the discard pile.



    Topic 10: Morale Boost

    Morale Boost is an ability displayed on a card below:
     

    Morale Boost is shown as a plus sign with two arrows pointing out on the left and right sides.

    When a unit with Morale Boost is played, it adds +1 power to all other units in the same row/section as it.



    Topic 11: Hero

    Hero cards are some of the most powerful in the game, however they have drawbacks. A hero card is shown below:
     

    Hero cards are important characters in the story of The Witcher series, such as the main man Geralt of Rivia. Hero cards can belong to either of the four factions, and have the same coloured banner that normal units in that faction have, or be neutral and be put into any deck. Hero cards are recognised by their power value being inside an orange sun shape with an orange design on the top left of the card.

    Hero cards can be for brute force, like Geralt of Riva, or have abilities like normal units, such as Yennefer of Vengerberg and the Medic ability.

    However, as previously mentioned, Hero cards have drawbacks; they cannot be affected by any cards, not a single one. This will be explained below:
    • If a Commander's Horn ability is in effect in the same section as a Hero, the Hero's power will not be doubled.
    • If a Scorch ability is used and the Hero would normally be the target, it will move to the next viable candidate.
    • Decoy cannot be used to swap a Hero.
    • Medic cannot revive a Hero.
    • Morale Boost does not give a Hero +1.



    Topic 12: Commander

    Each player has a card representing their faction's commander, with an example shown below:
     

    Each commander card has a unique that can be used once per match, by pressing  and then selecting the card.

    HURRAY!
    You have now read about every mechanic in Gwent, now on to card collecting!

    Preparation

    Ensure you always have the recommended collection tracking website, gwentcards.com , open at all times when playing The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt and going for this trophy. It is an excellent website, one I used to keep track of my collection throughout my time earning every card. 

    The website has a record of every card in the game, and as long as you don't clear your computer's cache will keep your progress in the checklist when you close your computer and turn it back on the next day. For any cards that have duplicates, the website specifices where each is located so you know you are ticking the correct copy of a card off the list.

    I will split this guide up into five sections representing the five major areas in the game that contain Gwent cards. I have them in the order I personally visited them throughout my natural story progression. Of course, if you decide to go to Skellige before Novigrad, more power to you, but I personally chose Novigrad first. Just swap steps 4 and 5 if you decide to do this.

    In every main "Step" of the collection process in this guide (apart from Step 2) I will include a map from gosunoob. These are maps of White Orchard, different parts of Velen, Novigrad, and the different Skellige Isles. Each map has numbered locations indicating Gwent players and/or sellers. Directly below each map I will explain what each number represents. I will state if the numbered person sells cards, and if they do not sell cards, I will just state who they are, so you know all you have to do is play them for a random card.

    If you play a single merchant/crafter more than once, they will not give you any more cards; you play a merchant/crafter once and they give you a card, but playing that same person again will not reward a card. Also if you notice that you start getting materials instead of a card from a merchant/trader you have not previously faced, move on to the next Step in the guide; there are more than enough merchants/traders to get you every single random card, including duplicates.

    You can make a manual save before facing a merchant/trader so if the card you get as a reward is a duplicate, you can load your save if you want a unique card instead of a duplicate. This just makes getting the trophy a bit quicker. I personally got all duplicates as well, so I never loaded a manual save.

    When you get to a new main area in the game, quickly look over its respective step in this guide; some cards are missable which I will designate with MISSABLE!

    With the new 1.20 update, there is now a specialised item in the game to help you with this trophy. This item is known as A Miraculous Guide to Gwent, and is a book that details what cards you are missing with instructions on how to obtain them. This book is either obtained from your very first Gwent opponent or, if you missed that, from a merchant in the Gildorf district of Novigrad (merchant is located slightly south-east of the St. Gregory's Bridge signpost in Novigrad). This book does not give you an order for the cards, however, so it is possible to miss a lot, so use that item in tandem with this guide.

    Below that, however, I will give a recommended order to go to each person, seeing as a lot of people are tied to quests and a lot of cards are extremely missable. Without further ado, I shall see you in Step 1!

    Step 1: White Orchard

    After the prologue in Kaer Morhen, your first main area will be White Orchard. 

    As previously stated, cards are split up into two categories; those obtained from very specific sellers or quests, and those obtained randomly from defeating merchants, traders, blacksmiths, armorers etc. 

    This guide will give the locations for the four different leader cards of Foltest, Emhyr, Eredin, and Francesca. Whilst playing my game they unlocked in a different order to other pages online, so I'll mention at each location that you will get 1/4 possible cards for that leader. All sixteen of these leader cards have set locations, so even though you unlock them in a weird order, you will get them all.

    You start the game with one card for each of the four faction leaders (Foltest, Emhyr var Emreis, Eredin, Francesca Findabair), and a base deck containing Biting Frost x2, Clear Weather, Impenetrable Fog x2, Torrential Rain, Ballista, Blue Stripes Commando x2, Dethmold, Dun Banner Medic, Kaedweni Siege Expert x3, Keira Metz, Poor Fucking Infantry x2, Prince Stennis, Redanian Foot Soldier x2, Sabrina Glevissig, Sheldon Skaggs, Siegfried of Denesle, Síle de Tansarville, Trebuchet x2, Ves, and Yarpen Zigrin. The game essentially gives you a starting Northern Realms deck.

    Once you reach the White Orchard Tavern with Vesemir, your Gwent collecting begins. This is the tavern with the bar maid named Elsa, so you know you're in the correct place.

    There are two (three technically) people of interest in the whole of White Orchard, so no need to sweat here!

    Places of Interest:

    1. Inside the White Orchard Tavern is a scholar, essentially serving as the introductory person for the game of Gwent. Defeat this opponent using your newfound knowledge gained from this guide to obtain the Zoltan Chivay card. From user HellcoreFire: (Don't know if this is still useful information but the Zoltan Chivay card is not missable anymore. If you lose from or don't play the scholar in White Orchard you can now find the card in Velen. Fast travel to Hanged Man's Tree. Next to the tree you will see a camp fire, use your witcher sense and you'll see a sack with the card and an unfinished book in it. I started playing the game again on my save file from 2015 and it was the only card I missed so I can confirm this is how I got it.)
    2. The inkeeper of White Orchard Tavern, Elsa, sells a few cards. The cards you can buy from her are Blue Stripes Commando, Catapult, Crinfrid Reavers Dragon Hunter, a Foltest leader card, and Decoy.
    3. A certain event will happen around the time of your visit to White Orchard Tavern that will prevent you from purchasing cards from Elsa. If you forget to buy them and this event occurs, do not panic, as the merchant outside by the signpost will sell the cards instead. This is the merchant you saved from the griffin.


    Roadmap:

    • STEP 1. When you reach White Orchard Tavern, find the scholar sitting at a table (1 on map) and play him for your very first Gwent win.
    • STEP 2. Talk to Elsa the inkeep (2 on map) immediately after defeating the scholar until you can look at what she is selling. Buy every card she has.
    • STEP 3 (OPTIONAL). If you reach the story event that makes Elsa's stock un-viewable, leave White Orchard Tavern and go to the merchant you saved from the griffin (3 on map) who is standing near the fast travel signpost. He will sell the exact same cards Elsa was.

    Step 2: Royal Palace in Vizima

    You will go to the Royal Palace in Vizima after leaving White Orchard.

    This section is relatively short but does contain one person of interest for Gwent.

    When in the courtyard on your way to leave the palace, there will be a Nilfgaardian nobleman sitting on a bench, and if recalled correctly, he has an (!) over his head. Defeat him and get an Emhyr var Emreis leader card.


    Roadmap:

    • STEP 1. After finishing your conversation with a very powerful individual and catching up with an old friend, you will have to leave the Royal Palace. Before you do, however, talk to the Nilfgaardian nobleman in the courtyard and defeat him.

    Step 3: Velen - No Man's Land

    Okay, now whereas White Orchard and the Royal Palace were kind of polite in only making you do a few things for cards, Velen is where it gets ramped up. Velen is one of the game's very large areas, covering a huge open landscape, numerous villages and boroughs, and also includes the fairly decently sized city of Oxenfurt.

    Throughout Velen, and Oxenfurt, there are numerous sellers selling very specific cards (which will be mentioned) and a large number of opportunities to play merchants and other crafters to get a card from the random card pool. Velen also offers the first Gwent specific quest, which has unique and powerful cards tied to it.

    Two of the merchants/crafters you can defeat in Velen are, annoyingly, not in their respective locations when the game begins, but they are marked on the map where they should be. A lot of people on the Internet freaked out about this, including myself at first, but they are both there, you must just complete a task to get them. Fear not, friend, as details will be included about each in the roadmap.

    Now that you are beginning the arduous task of defeating random merchants/crafters for random cards, you will notice sometimes that a person is not where they should be, even if they are marked on the in-game map, such as a blacksmith not being at his anvil. Worry not; simply meditate for a while and repeat this until they appear. Sometimes they will not talk to you, so just repeat the previously mentioned meditation method. Also ensure you are trying to talk to the correct person; sometimes a map may say a blacksmith in village X, and said village may have two blacksmiths beside each other, and only one plays Gwent. So just be careful.

    Roadmap:

    • STEP 1. This map does not include a very key location, so I will put it first on the roadmap so you do not forget it. There is an inn called Inn at the Crossroads located on the middle island located north-east of Crow's Perch.



     

    • You will probably come here naturally, but just wanted to use to map on the off chance you did not. Go to Inn at the Crossroads first, ignoring all other Gwent related things, and purchase the cards from the inkeep. They sell Impera Brigade Guard, Nausicaa Cavalry Rider, Siege Engineer, an Emhyr var Emreis leader card, and Commander's Horn.
    • STEP 2. Work through every number (1 - 13) on the list under the map above, buying any cards mentioned and defeating every numbered person who plays Gwent. The only numbers you should not go for during this step are 3 and 12, for reason explained in further steps.
    • STEP 3. Once you have successfully worked through the previous step and gathered a large number of cards, we will be going for person 12. This person is a craftsman/trader in the village of Claywich, however they are not currently in the village at all. If you look at the 12 label on the map, which shows where the trader will be, you will see an island to the south-east, connected to the mainland by a little bridge. If you look at this island on your in-game map, you will notice a ? on it. This is a person in distress, and that person is, of course, our Gwent player from Claywich. Make a manual save before tackling this, and go in, kill the bandits, loot the key from one of their bodies and free the Claywich resident. After you save him he will return to Claywich, so return there, meditate for a bit if need be, and talk to him for your Gwent rewards as mentioned above. If, for some reason, you kill all the bandits and none drop the key to the cage, reload your manual save and try again.
    • STEP 4. The main story in Velen will lead you to Crow's Perch (home to people 4, 5, 6, and 7) and the Bloody Baron. You will converse numerous times with the Bloody Baron for the main story, so this one is very easy to get. When you are able to freely talk to the Baron, do so and ask if he plays Gwent. The Baron is the first real tough opponent you will face, and defeating him will award you the unique card Sigismund Dijkstra. Defeating him will also start the quest Gwent: Velen Players. Once you receive this quest, you will be faced with defeating three further opponents: man in Oreton, Haddy of Midcopse, and the soothsayer in Benek. Defeating these further three opponents will reward you with Letho of Gulet, Vernon Roche, and Crone: Weavess. You will also receive an Eredin leader card for completing the overall quest. A certain point may occur during the story which prevents you from playing against the Bloody Baron, so it is recommended you face him as soon as you can. If this event occurs, however, Sigismund Dijkstra will be sitting on his desk.

Buy Stjepan the inkeep's cards in the Alchemy Inn, and then face him as part of this quest. He rewards you with the unique card Yennefer of Vengerberg, and prompts you to face two further opponents in this quest; the inkeep in the Inn at the Crossroads for the card Menno Coehoorn, and Olivier in the Kingfisher in Novigrad for Tibor Eggebracht. No need to worry about Olivier in Kingfisher; we're not at Novigrad just yet, but return to the Inn at the Crossroads and defeat the inkeep. You may have potentially done this already during your first visit to the Inn at the Crossroads, but if not you can do it now.
    • STEP 2. Tackle people 2, 4, and 5; the other three players in Oxenfurt.
    • STEP 3. Travel north-east of Oxenfurt to face the dwarven herbalist. Meditate if he is not there.

Make a manual save before killing bandits just in case none of them drop a key.

    Roadmap:

    • STEP 1. Play through Novigrad's main story missions as you normally would until you complete the main mission Broken Flowers. After completion of this quest, you can challenge your old pal Zoltan Chivay to a match of Gwent just outside/inside the Rosemary & Thyme inn. Defeating him will award you the card Eithné and will kick off the quest Gwent: Old Pals, but the remainder of that quest can be put on hold for the time being.
    • STEP 2. Wander about the city of Novigrad and its surrounding area and take care of every person you can, defeating those who will face you, and buying cards of those who sell them. The only people you should not concern yourself with in this step are 2 (Marquise Serenity), 12 (Olivier), 17 (Vimmi Vivaldi), 21 (Zoltan which you got in Step 1), and 24 (Scoia'tael merchant).
    • STEP 3. Defeat Vimmi Vivaldi (marked as 17 on map) in his bank to get the unique Vesemir card and start the quest Gwent: Big City Players. After defeating Vivaldi, go to Marquise Serenity in the Passiflora brothel (marked as 2 on map), buy her cards and then defeat her as part of the quest to get her unique Morvran Voorhis card. Marquise Serenity will tell you about the next opponent in the quest who, if you are following this guide, will not be available and the quest will appear as completed (even though it technically isn't). Do not worry; this will be explained in a further step, so just continue the guide.
    • STEP 4. Buy all cards from Olivier (marked 12 on map) and defeat him as part of Gwent: Playing Inkeeps to win his unique Tibor Eggebracht. If Olivier is not there for any reason, the card can be collected in the back room next to the bar.
    • STEP 5 (MISSABLE!). One of the first major characters from The Witcher series you will encounter in Novigrad is Triss Merigold. Triss is involved in a lot of major story missions, but also has her own series of side quests, eventually leading to romancing her. Even if you have no plans to romance Triss, or want to romance Yennfer instead (my choice), you still have to do Triss' quest line to get a few cards. After completing the main story mission Get Junior which shortly follows Broken Flowers mentioned in the previous stop, you will unlock the mission Redania's Most Wanted. Complete this side mission once you unlock it. If you already have the side mission An Eye for an Eye unlocked by some strange means, do that now after completing Redania's Most Wanted. If you do not have An Eye for an Eye unlocked, continue playing main story missions until you finish A Poet Under Pressure, which will unlock An Eye for an Eye. After completing both Redania's Most Wanted and An Eye for an Eye, you will unlock the Triss side mission A Matter of Life and Death. Begin this mission, and during it you will go to a masquerade ball with Triss. Before speaking to your target in the ball, go to the nearby yellow ! quest marker to partake in a three round Gwent tournament. You can manually save between each round so no need to worry. Defeating the opponents in this tournamet will award you Vampire: Bruxa, Milva, and Dandelion.
    • STEP 6 (MISSABLE!). When you return Novigrad, seek out Dijkstra in his bath house and play him as part of Gwent: Big City Players (which is now available again) to win his unique Esterad Thyssen card. He will then direct you to the Scoia'tael merchant (marked as 24) on map. Go there and defeat him as the last opponent in this quest to win the unique Cirilla Fiona Elen Riannon. You will also be awarded a Francesca Finadabair leader card for completing Gwent: Big City Players.
    • STEP 7. After completing A Matter of Life and Death, you will shortly receive the side mission Now or Never, the final mission in Triss' line of side quests. Complete this side quest, making decisions at the end as to whether you wish Triss to be your romantic interest or not. After this quest ends, Dijkstra will give you a mission called Deadly Plot. Complete this mission and you will rescue Thaler at the very end. Immediately after saving Thaler, you will get the quest Gwent: Playing Thaler. Defeat Thaler there where you save him and get the unique Geralt of Rivia card (hey, that's you!). Apparently if you do not defeat Thaler here, he will make his way to the Seven Cats Inn, marked as 8 on the above map.
    • STEP 8. Continue tracking Gwent: Old Pals and go defeat Vernon Roche, who is now available to face after completing An Eye for an Eye. Defeat Roche and gain the unique Saesenthessis card.
    • STEP 9 (MISSABLE!). The quest Gwent: Old Pals requires you to defeat your old friend Lambert, who can be quite tricky to track down. Check notice boards around Novigrad (you may already have it) until you get Contract: Monster in the Bits or, even better, Following the Thread. Following the Thread is a side quest involving Lambert, which can be obtained through Contract: Monster in the Bits if you do not already have it. Progress through this quest until you and Lambert decide to split up to track down an individual each. The person you are tracking is actually on Skellige, so we will pick this up when we are there, but we at least need to have gotten to this part in the quest first.
    • STEP 10 (MISSABLE!). Return to the Rosemary & Thyme inn to find Zoltan being harassed. Agree to help him and you will start the quest A Dangerous Game. This is a Gwent themed side mission, even if its name does not contain the word Gwent, and involves you tracking down three rare, unique cards. At the very end of the mission, Zoltan will allow you to keep the cards or trade them for money. You MUST keep the cards, otherwise you will fail the entire trophy.

    Step 6: Finale

    Now that your deck is probably destroying opponents, it's time get your final cards. Begin tracking the quest High Stakes, which is obtained from a notice board in Novigrad, and head to the Passiflora brothel in Novigrad. Ensure you are confident with your deck and have at least 1,000 gold.

    In this tournament, you pay 1,000 gold as a buy-in, and must face four opponents in Gwent, each awarding you a unique card. Assuming you have bought every card mentioned previously and have played enough merchants that you have received every random card, all you should be missing are one last card for Foltest, Emhyr var Emreis, Eredin, and Francesca Findabair, all of which are obtained in this quest.

    You can make a manual save before the first, second, and last opponent, but not between the second and third, so you have to defeat them both in a row. The mission has a recommended level of 26 because you may get into a fist fight with a pretty tricky opponent after defeating the third opponent, so you have to beat two opponents in Gwent and win a tough fist fight all in a row.

    After defeating Tybalt, your final opponent, you will receive your final leader card and the trophy should pop! Congratulations!
